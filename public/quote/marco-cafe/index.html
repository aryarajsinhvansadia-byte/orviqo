A price you can check, line by line.

Below is exactly what makes up the total, what each part involves, and why it's worth paying for. If any line doesn't earn its place for you, say so and we'll take it out.

Design & art direction

₹ 4,000

A layout designed around your photographs — colour, typography and the overall feel, drawn up for your café specifically. Includes two rounds of changes.

Why it costs what it does: a ready-made template is cheaper, and it also looks like the other forty cafés using it. This is the part that makes someone scrolling past stop and think that place looks good.

Building the five pages

₹ 5,000

Home, Menu, About, Gallery and Visit — written, built and tested on real phone, tablet and desktop screens, with the page speed tuned so it opens almost instantly.

Why it costs what it does: this is the largest single piece of work — five distinct page layouts, the words on them, and testing across the screen sizes your customers actually use. Roughly seven in ten people will see this site on a phone while standing outside deciding where to eat.

Menu presentation & updates

₹ 2,000

Your menu laid out so it reads well on a small screen, and built so you can change a price or add a dish yourself — no developer, no waiting.

Why it costs what it does: a café menu changes. A website that can't keep up is wrong within a month, and a wrong price on a website is an argument at the counter.

Getting found on Google

₹ 2,000

Page titles and descriptions written for search, your business details marked up so Google can read them, Google Maps embedded, and your Google Business Profile connected to the site.

Why it costs what it does: this is what turns the website from a brochure into a source of walk-ins. Without it you have a site that only people who already know you will ever see.

Launch & first 30 days of care

Domain and hosting connected, the site taken live, and a full month afterwards where any small change — a photo, a price, an opening hour — is made free of charge.

Why it costs what it does: launch is where things break, and the first month is when you'll spot what you want changed. Both are covered rather than billed as extras.

04 — Recommended next

What we'd do next, in the order we'd do it.

None of these are required — the website works completely without them. These are the four things that would make the most difference once you're live, in the order we'd tackle them, priced upfront.

Recommended first

Google Business Profile

₹ 3,000 one-time

Your Google listing set up properly and matched to the website: name, address and hours identical, the right categories, your photos, and the menu linked. Ongoing management — posts, photo updates, replying to reviews — available at ₹ 1,500 / month.

Why this one first: when someone nearby searches "café near me", Google shows the map listing before it shows any website. That listing brings in more walk-ins than the site will on its own. The website's job is to convince someone once they've found you — this is what gets you found.

Photography — half day at the café

₹ 6,000 one-time

A half day photographing the space, the coffee and the food, shot to fit the layouts the site is built around.

Why it matters: the design is deliberately photo-forward — large images, few words, the atmosphere doing the selling. Your own phone photos will work. Good photographs are what make that design do its job, and they're reusable across Instagram, Google and delivery apps.

Care plan, after the free first month

₹ 1,000 / month

Hosting kept running, the site kept updated and secure, and small changes — a new dish, a price, a holiday closure — made for you whenever you need them.

Why it matters: a café changes constantly. Without this the site still runs perfectly well and you can edit the menu yourself; this is for when you'd rather send a message than do it.

Online table booking

from ₹ 6,000 one-time

Guests reserve a table from the website, and you see the bookings on your phone.

Why it's last: only worth it if you actually take reservations. If Marco Café is walk-in, this adds a button nobody presses — we'd rather tell you that than sell it.
