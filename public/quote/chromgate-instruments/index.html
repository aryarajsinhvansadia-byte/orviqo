03 — The specification assistant

The part your competitors don't have.

A chat assistant powered by Anthropic's Claude, the same AI used by Fortune 500 companies — but restricted entirely to the Chromgate catalogue. It works the way one of your sales engineers would.

It asks the right questions

Which instruments, which gases, what the method must detect — then recommends real Chromgate products, with links.

It cannot make things up

It will not quote a price, a lead time or a certification. Asked for any of those, it routes the customer to your technical team.

It stays up

It runs on three separate AI models. If one is busy, the next takes over, so a visitor is never left without an answer.

It qualifies the enquiry

By the time someone contacts you, they've already described their instruments and gases — so your team starts halfway through the conversation.

04 — Where every rupee goes

A price you can check, line by line.

Below is exactly what makes up the total, what each part involved, and why it's worth paying for. If any line doesn't earn its place for you, say so and we'll discuss it.

The website — ₹ 15,000

Design & art direction

₹ 4,000

A design language created for Chromgate: the type, the rules, the specification tables, the numbered figures — all drawn up specifically, then applied consistently across 118 pages.

Why it costs what it does: a template would have been faster and cheaper, and it would have looked like every other instrument supplier in India. Your customers are technical buyers who judge a supplier partly on whether they look precise. This is that impression.

Building and writing 118 pages

₹ 5,000

72 individual product pages with specification tables, 6 range pages, 8 industries, 8 applications, 6 technical articles and 18 company pages — every word written from your catalogue, none of it placeholder.

Why it costs what it does: this is the largest single piece of work. Each product page is a separate page Google can rank — 72 chances to be found rather than one. A single-page site would have cost less and been invisible.

Recovering 73 product photographs

₹ 2,500

Your catalogue was a print PDF with no separate image files. Each product photograph was located inside the pages, cut out, cleaned, centred on a consistent background and converted to a modern web format.

Why it costs what it does: the alternative was a photoshoot of 73 products before the site could launch. This got you live now, and you can replace them with original photography whenever you choose.

The search foundation

₹ 3,500

Every product, your company, your two Vadodara locations and your FAQs marked up in the format Google reads directly; an image sitemap; local search consistency; six keyword clusters and a 106-article plan.

Why it costs what it does: this is what turns the site from a brochure into a source of enquiries. Without it, only people who already know Chromgate would ever see it.

The AI specification assistant — ₹ 15,000

Teaching it your catalogue — and its limits

₹ 6,000

The assistant is given a document compiled from the same product data the website displays, so the two can never disagree. On top of that sit the rules: never state a specification that isn't in the catalogue, never quote a price or lead time, never claim a certification.

Why it costs what it does: this is the difference between an asset and a liability. An AI that invents a pressure rating or claims an accreditation you don't hold creates a real commercial problem. Most of this work is the guardrails, not the answers.

The chat interface

₹ 4,000

The panel itself, matched to the site's design, working on desktop and on phones, with the answers streaming in as they're written rather than making the visitor wait.

Why it costs what it does: a slow or awkward chat box gets closed. This one is reachable from every page, including the mobile action bar.

Making it reliable

₹ 5,000

Three separate AI models arranged in sequence so a busy period never leaves a visitor stranded, plus rate limiting against misuse, a hard spending cap, and testing against the questions most likely to cause trouble.

Why it costs what it does: an AI that fails in front of a customer is worse than no AI. It was deliberately tested by trying to make it misbehave — asking it for prices, for certifications, for specifications that don't exist.
